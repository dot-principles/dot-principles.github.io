# Disclaimer

This repository is a **proof of concept**.

## Status

- There will be errors - in principle descriptions, citations, layer assignments, and group compositions.
- Groupings are opinionated. The selection of which principles belong together, and which are Layer 1 vs. Layer 2 vs. Layer 3, reflects particular judgments that others may reasonably disagree with.
- Many adjustments are still needed: coverage gaps exist, some namespaces are incomplete, and the catalog is not exhaustive.

## Contributions are welcome

If you find an error, a better source, a missing principle, or a group that doesn't fit your context - please open a pull request. See [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md#13-contributing-principles) for the contribution process.

> **Scope:** Contributed principles must be **established, widely recognized concepts** from the software engineering literature - named principles, published patterns, or documented practices backed by authoritative sources. They must not duplicate what is already in the catalog. If your principle is original, company-specific, or niche, keep it in your own **extra catalog** instead (see [INSTALL.md §10](INSTALL.md#10-installing-an-extra-catalog)). No fork is needed.

## Use this as a base

You are encouraged to extend this catalog for your own context with an extra catalog (or a fork, if you want to change shipped content):

- **Corporate principles** - add a namespace (e.g., `acme/`) with your organization's engineering standards
- **Domain-specific principles** - add a namespace for your domain (e.g., `fintech/`, `embedded/`)
- **Opinionated company groups** - define groups that combine or suppress shipped principles to fit your team's approach

The `.principles` system is designed to be extended. Nothing here is authoritative or mandatory - it is a starting point.

> **Namespace ownership:** Original files you create in your own namespace (e.g., `acme/`, `mycompany/`) are your own work, not adaptations of this project's content. Only modifications to existing shipped principle files are considered adaptations under CC BY-SA 4.0.
